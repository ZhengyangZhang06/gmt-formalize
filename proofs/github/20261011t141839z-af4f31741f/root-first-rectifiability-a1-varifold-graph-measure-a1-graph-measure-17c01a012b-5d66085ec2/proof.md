## Proof

Source: Step 5 of the parent proof (extension from rectangles with finite-weight base to all Borel subsets of ℝ^N × G(N,n), using σ-finiteness of μ_V), stated for general measurable spaces. It is the standard uniqueness theorem for measures that agree on a generating π-system containing a countable cover by sets of finite measure (Dynkin's π-λ theorem; cf. Billingsley, Probability and Measure, §10; Mathlib: MeasureTheory.Measure.ext_of_generateFrom_of_iUnion, Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Restrict.lean:491).

Hypotheses. (X, 𝒜) and (Y, ℬ) are measurable spaces, and X × Y carries the product σ-algebra 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ. μ is a σ-finite measure on X, and ρ₁, ρ₂ are measures on X × Y. Moreover:
 (F) for every A ∈ 𝒜 with μ(A) ≠ ∞, ρ₁(A × Y) ≠ ∞;
 (R) for every A ∈ 𝒜 with μ(A) ≠ ∞ and every B ∈ ℬ, ρ₁(A × B) = ρ₂(A × B).
Goal: ρ₁ = ρ₂.

1. Spanning sets. Since μ is σ-finite, the sets E_m := spanningSets μ m (m ∈ ℕ) satisfy:
 - E_m ∈ 𝒜 (measurableSet_spanningSets);
 - μ(E_m) < ∞ (measure_spanningSets_lt_top);
 - ⋃_m E_m = X (iUnion_spanningSets).

2. The generating class. Let 𝒞 := {A × B : A ∈ 𝒜, B ∈ ℬ, μ(A) ≠ ∞}.

3. 𝒞 is a π-system. Let A × B and A' × B' be in 𝒞. Then (A × B) ∩ (A' × B') = (A ∩ A') × (B ∩ B') (Set.prod_inter_prod). Here A ∩ A' ∈ 𝒜 and B ∩ B' ∈ ℬ, and μ(A ∩ A') ≤ μ(A) < ∞ by monotonicity. So the intersection lies in 𝒞.

4. 𝒞 generates 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ.
 (a) σ(𝒞) ⊆ 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ. Each member A × B of 𝒞 is a measurable rectangle, so it is 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ-measurable (MeasurableSet.prod). Hence σ(𝒞) ⊆ 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ (MeasurableSpace.generateFrom_le).
 (b) 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ ⊆ σ(𝒞). The σ-algebra 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ is generated by the measurable rectangles A × B with A ∈ 𝒜, B ∈ ℬ (generateFrom_prod), so it suffices to show each such rectangle lies in σ(𝒞). Since ⋃_m E_m = X, we have A × B = (⋃_m (A ∩ E_m)) × B = ⋃_m ((A ∩ E_m) × B) (Set.inter_iUnion, Set.iUnion_prod_const). For each m, A ∩ E_m ∈ 𝒜 and μ(A ∩ E_m) ≤ μ(E_m) < ∞, so (A ∩ E_m) × B ∈ 𝒞 ⊆ σ(𝒞). A countable union of members of σ(𝒞) lies in σ(𝒞), so A × B ∈ σ(𝒞).
 Together, (a) and (b) give 𝒜 ⊗ ℬ = σ(𝒞) (le_antisymm).

5. A countable cover of finite ρ₁-measure. For m ∈ ℕ put C_m := E_m × Y.
 - C_m ∈ 𝒞, because E_m ∈ 𝒜, Y ∈ ℬ and μ(E_m) ≠ ∞.
 - ⋃_m C_m = (⋃_m E_m) × Y = X × Y (Set.iUnion_prod_const, Set.univ_prod_univ).
 - ρ₁(C_m) ≠ ∞, by (F) with A = E_m.

6. Agreement on 𝒞. For every A × B ∈ 𝒞, ρ₁(A × B) = ρ₂(A × B) by (R).

7. Conclusion. ρ₁ and ρ₂ agree on the π-system 𝒞 (steps 3 and 6), and 𝒞 generates the σ-algebra of X × Y (step 4). Also, 𝒞 contains the countable cover (C_m) of X × Y with ρ₁(C_m) < ∞ (step 5). By the uniqueness theorem for measures that agree on such a π-system, ρ₁ = ρ₂. In Lean this is MeasureTheory.Measure.ext_of_generateFrom_of_iUnion applied to 𝒞 and B := (m ↦ E_m ×ˢ univ), with hypotheses: generation (step 4), π-system (step 3), the union (step 5), membership (step 5), finiteness (step 5) and agreement (step 6). ∎

## Key steps

1. E_m := spanningSets μ m are measurable, μ(E_m) < ∞, and ⋃_m E_m = X.
2. 𝒞 := {A × B : A, B measurable, μ(A) < ∞} is a π-system (prod_inter_prod, measure_mono).
3. 𝒞 generates the product σ-algebra: A × B = ⋃_m (A ∩ E_m) × B together with generateFrom_prod.
4. C_m := E_m × Y ∈ 𝒞 cover X × Y, and ρ₁(C_m) < ∞ by the cylinder hypothesis.
5. ρ₁ and ρ₂ agree on 𝒞; Measure.ext_of_generateFrom_of_iUnion gives ρ₁ = ρ₂.
