## Proof

Source: step 2 of the parent proof (scaling). It is generalized from a ball to an open set U and from B_1 to a target ball B_r(z0), so that it also gives the blow-up equation of the parent's step 5e on D_k = B_{1/(2 rho_k)}(0).
0. Let T(z) := y + rho z. T is an affine homeomorphism of R^n with inverse T^{-1}(x) = rho^{-1}(x - y). Since |T(z) - T(z0)| = rho |z - z0|, T maps B_r(z0) onto B_{rho r}(y + rho z0), which is contained in U. If r <= 0, the ball B_r(z0) is empty and (i)-(iii) are trivial (both integrals in (iii) are 0).
1. (i), (ii). Let z be in B_r(z0). Then T(z) is in U, and U is open, so w is differentiable at T(z). T is differentiable with DT = rho Id. By the chain rule, wt = w o T is differentiable at z with Dwt(z) = Dw(T z) o (rho Id) = rho Dw(T z). Moreover, wt is C^1 on B_r(z0), as the composition of the C^1 map w on U with the smooth map T, which sends B_r(z0) into U.
2. Transferring the test function. Let zetat be C^1 with compact support and tsupport zetat contained in B_r(z0). Define zeta := zetat o T^{-1}, i.e. zeta(x) = zetat(rho^{-1}(x - y)). Then zeta is C^1, zetat = zeta o T, and Dzetat(z) = rho Dzeta(T z) for every z (chain rule). Since T is a homeomorphism, tsupport zeta = T(tsupport zetat). This set is compact and contained in T(B_r(z0)), which is contained in U. Hence zeta is admissible, and (*) the integral over U of F equals the integral over U of G, where F(x) := a(x)[Dw(x)][Dzeta(x)] and G(x) := Phi(x)[Dzeta(x)].
3. Vanishing off the supports. If x is not in tsupport zeta, then zeta vanishes near x, so Dzeta(x) = 0. By linearity of a(x)[P][.] and of Phi(x), F(x) = G(x) = 0. Since tsupport zeta is contained in U, the integral over U of F equals the integral over R^n of F, and likewise for G (a set integral of a function vanishing outside the set equals the whole integral). Put Ft(z) := a(T z)[Dwt(z)][Dzetat(z)] and Gt(z) := (rho Phi(T z))[Dzetat(z)]. In the same way they vanish outside tsupport zetat, which is contained in B_r(z0), so their integrals over B_r(z0) equal their integrals over R^n.
4. Pointwise identities: Ft(z) = rho^2 F(T z) and Gt(z) = rho^2 G(T z) for every z in R^n. If z is in B_r(z0), then by (ii) and step 2, Ft(z) = a(T z)[rho Dw(T z)][rho Dzeta(T z)] = rho^2 F(T z) by bilinearity, and Gt(z) = rho Phi(T z)[rho Dzeta(T z)] = rho^2 G(T z). If z is not in B_r(z0), then z is not in tsupport zetat, so Dzetat(z) = 0. Also T z is not in T(tsupport zetat) = tsupport zeta, so Dzeta(T z) = 0. All four quantities are then 0.
5. Change of variables. For every function H : R^n -> R, the integral over R^n of H(y + rho z) dz equals rho^{-n} times the integral of H(x) dx. This holds without any integrability assumption, because both sides are 0 when H is not integrable. Indeed, translation invariance of Lebesgue measure (Mathlib: integral_add_left_eq_self) and the scaling formula for Haar measure (Mathlib: MeasureTheory.Measure.integral_comp_smul, which gives the factor |rho^n|^{-1}) hold for arbitrary functions.
6. Conclusion. Using steps 3, 4 and 5 and (*):
integral over B_r(z0) of Ft = integral of Ft = rho^2 (integral of F(T z) dz) = rho^{2-n} (integral of F) = rho^{2-n} (integral over U of F) = rho^{2-n} (integral over U of G) = rho^{2-n} (integral of G) = rho^2 (integral of G(T z) dz) = integral of Gt = integral over B_r(z0) of Gt.
Since Gt(z) = (rho Phi(y + rho z))[Dzetat(z)], this is (iii). QED

4. all integrands vanish off the test-function supports, so set integrals equal whole-space integrals
5. pointwise identities Ft = rho^2 F o T and Gt = rho^2 G o T
6. change of variables: integral of H(y + rho z) dz = rho^{-n} times integral of H, valid without integrability
7. combine with the weak equation for w
