## Proof

Source: step 2(c) of the parent proof, stated for a general weight f. That step differentiates the cutoff integrals I_eps and J_eps under the integral sign and shows that E_eps is continuous. In the application, f is the constant 1, x -> (x - y).H(x), or the conical weight nu.

Notation: r_x := |x - y|, phi' := deriv phi, B_S := closedBall y S. Note that S > T t > 0.

1. Neighbourhood.
 - Put t_1 := (t + S/T)/2. Since T t < S, we have t < t_1 < S/T, so T t_1 < S.
 - Let N := (t/2, t_1). This is an open interval containing t and contained in (0, infinity).

2. Vanishing.
 - phi = 0 on [T, infinity), so phi' = 0 on the open interval (T, infinity).
 - Let s be in N. If r_x > T s, then r_x/s > T, so phi(r_x/s) = 0 and phi'(r_x/s) = 0.
 - Hence, for s in N, each of x -> phi(r_x/s) f(x), x -> (r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s) f(x) and x -> phi'(r_x/s)(r_x/s^2) f(x) vanishes outside the closed ball B_(Ts)(y). That ball lies in B_S, because T s < T t_1 < S.

3. Bounds.
 - Let C_0 := max over [0, T] of |phi| and C_1 := max over [0, T] of |u phi'(u)|. Both are finite because phi and phi' are continuous (phi is C^1).
 - For u >= 0 we have |phi(u)| <= C_0 and |u phi'(u)| <= C_1, since both functions vanish for u > T.
 - So, for s in N and every x (note r_x/s >= 0):
   |phi(r_x/s) f(x)| <= C_0 |f(x)| 1_{B_S}(x),
   |(r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s) f(x)| <= C_1 |f(x)| 1_{B_S}(x),
   |phi'(r_x/s)(r_x/s^2) f(x)| = s^(-1)|(r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s)| |f(x)| <= (2/t) C_1 |f(x)| 1_{B_S}(x).
 - The function |f| 1_{B_S} is mu-integrable, because f is integrable on B_S.

4. Measurability.
 - f is a.e.-strongly measurable for mu restricted to B_S.
 - Fix s in N. Each integrand in step 2 is a continuous function of x times f on B_S, and is zero outside B_S.
 - So it is the indicator of the measurable set B_S applied to a function that is a.e.-strongly measurable for mu restricted to B_S. Hence it is a.e.-strongly measurable for mu.
 - Together with step 3, all these integrands are mu-integrable.

5. Dependence on s for fixed x.
 - s -> phi(r_x/s) and s -> (r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s) are continuous on (0, infinity), as compositions of continuous maps.
 - By the chain rule, s -> phi(r_x/s) is differentiable on (0, infinity) with derivative phi'(r_x/s)(-r_x/s^2) = -s^(-1)(r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s).

6. Parts (a) and (b). Use the dominated convergence theorem for integrals depending continuously on a parameter:
 - domination by C_0|f|1_{B_S}, resp. C_1|f|1_{B_S}, for all s in the neighbourhood N of t (step 3);
 - measurability from step 4;
 - continuity in s from step 5.
 Hence s -> integral phi(r_x/s) f(x) dmu and s -> integral (r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s) f(x) dmu are continuous at t.

7. Part (c). Use differentiation under the integral sign. For every s in N:
 - the s-derivative -s^(-1)(r_x/s) phi'(r_x/s) f(x) is bounded by the integrable function (2/t) C_1 |f| 1_{B_S} (step 3);
 - the integrands are a.e.-strongly measurable and integrable (step 4);
 - the pointwise derivative exists (step 5).
 So s -> integral phi(r_x/s) f(x) dmu is differentiable at t, with derivative integral -t^(-1)(r_x/t) phi'(r_x/t) f(x) dmu = -(integral (r_x/t) phi'(r_x/t) f(x) dmu)/t.

## Key steps

1. Choose t_1 in (t, S/T) and work on the neighbourhood N = (t/2, t_1).
2. For s in N, all integrands vanish outside B_(Ts)(y), which lies inside B_S(y).
3. |phi| <= C_0 and |u phi'(u)| <= C_1 on [0, infinity). This gives the dominating functions C|f|1_{B_S}, and (2/t)C_1|f|1_{B_S} for the s-derivative.
4. The integrands are a.e.-strongly measurable: each is the indicator of B_S of a continuous function times f.
5. Pointwise in x, the integrands are continuous in s, and s -> phi(r_x/s) has derivative -s^(-1)(r_x/s)phi'(r_x/s).
6. Dominated convergence gives (a) and (b); differentiation under the integral sign gives (c).
