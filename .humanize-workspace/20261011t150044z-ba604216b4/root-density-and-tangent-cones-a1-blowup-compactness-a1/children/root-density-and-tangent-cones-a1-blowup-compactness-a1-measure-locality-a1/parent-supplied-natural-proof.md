## Proof

Source: step 0.5 of the parent proof (its first assertion and consequence (ii)); Simon Ch. 6, 3.2(3) (chapter-06.txt lines 768–772: M_W(T) = ∫_{M∩W} θ dHⁿ for open W) and 2.16 (lines 335–337, equality of currents in an open set). Inherited dependency (a resolved dependency of the parent node): dtc_mass_eq_measure (M): if U is open, W' ⊆ U is open and R represents T ∈ 𝒟_n(U), then T.massOn W' = R.measure W'. No sibling is used.
Notation. For an IMRep R = (M, θ, ξ), R.measure = (Hⁿ.restrict M).withDensity θ, so R.measure(B) = ∫⁻_{B∩M} θ dHⁿ for Borel B. Put μ_i := R_i.measure (i = 1, 2).
1. (Masses of T₁ and T₂ agree on every open V ⊆ W.) Let V ⊆ W be open. By definition T_i.massOn V is the supremum of ofReal(T_i(ω)) over ω ∈ massAdmissibleOn U_i n V, i.e. over test forms ω in U_i with tsupport ω ⊆ V and |ω(y)| ≤ 1 for all y (Euclidean coefficient norm). A differential form ω with IsTestForm ω and tsupport ω ⊆ V belongs to TestFormOn U₁ n, to TestFormOn U₂ n and to TestFormOn W n, because V ⊆ W ⊆ U₁ ∩ U₂; hence the two admissible families consist of the same underlying forms. For such ω, T_i(ω) = currentEval T_i ω (definition of currentEval: the condition IsTestForm ω ∧ tsupport ω ⊆ U_i holds), and the hypothesis, applied to ω regarded as an element of TestFormOn W n, gives currentEval T₁ ω = currentEval T₂ ω. So the two sets {ofReal(T_i(ω))} coincide and T₁.massOn V = T₂.massOn V. By M (applied in U_i, which is open, to the open set V ⊆ U_i), μ₁(V) = T₁.massOn V = T₂.massOn V = μ₂(V).
2. (Finiteness on relatively compact open subsets of W.) If V is open and closure V is a compact subset of W, then μ_i(V) ≤ μ_i(closure V) = ∫⁻_{M_i ∩ closure V} θ_i dHⁿ < ∞ by the field locallyIntegrable_mult of R_i, since closure V is a compact subset of W ⊆ U_i.
3. (Exhaustion of W.) For q ∈ ℕ, q ≥ 1, let V_q := ball(0, q) ∖ cthickening(1/q, ℝ^P ∖ W) (so V_q = ball(0, q) if W = ℝ^P). V_q is open (an open set minus a closed set) and V_q ⊆ V_{q+1}. Its closure is contained in closedBall(0, q) ∖ thickening(1/q, ℝ^P ∖ W), a compact set contained in W (a point whose distance to ℝ^P ∖ W is at least 1/q does not lie in ℝ^P ∖ W). Finally ⋃_q V_q = W: if y ∈ W choose r > 0 with ball(y, r) ⊆ W; then dist(y, ℝ^P ∖ W) ≥ r, and y ∈ V_q as soon as q > |y| and 1/q < r.
4. (Equality on each V_q.) Fix q and let ν_i := μ_i.restrict V_q. By step 2 each ν_i is a finite measure. For every open O ⊆ ℝ^P, ν_i(O) = μ_i(O ∩ V_q) and O ∩ V_q is an open subset of W, so ν₁(O) = ν₂(O) by step 1; in particular ν₁(ℝ^P) = ν₂(ℝ^P). The Borel σ-algebra of ℝ^P is generated by the open sets, which form a π-system (Mathlib isPiSystem_isOpen), and two finite measures that agree on a generating π-system and on the whole space are equal (Mathlib MeasureTheory.ext_of_generate_finite). Hence ν₁ = ν₂.
5. (Conclusion.) Let B ⊆ ℝ^P be Borel. Since the open sets V_q increase to W, continuity from below gives μ_i(B ∩ W) = lim_q μ_i(B ∩ V_q) = lim_q ν_i(B), and these limits coincide by step 4. Therefore (μ₁.restrict W)(B) = μ₁(B ∩ W) = μ₂(B ∩ W) = (μ₂.restrict W)(B) for every measurable B, i.e. μ₁.restrict W = μ₂.restrict W. ∎
Remark (how the siblings use this). With U₁ = U₂ = W = U and T₁ = T₂ = T one gets R₁.measure.restrict U = R₂.measure.restrict U for two representations of the same T ∈ 𝒟_n(U); since the carriers lie in U, R_i.measure.restrict U = R_i.measure (Mathlib restrict_withDensity and Measure.restrict_restrict), so the weight μ_T := R.measure does not depend on the representation (Simon 6.3.2(3)). This consequence was checked in Lean from the exact type above.

## Key steps

1. For open V ⊆ W the admissible forms for M_V(T₁) and M_V(T₂) are the same forms and T₁, T₂ agree on them, so M_V(T₁) = M_V(T₂); by dtc_mass_eq_measure μ₁(V) = μ₂(V).
2. μ_i(V) < ∞ whenever closure V is a compact subset of W (local integrability of θ_i).
3. Exhaust W by open V_q ↑ W with compact closures in W; the finite measures μ_i⌞V_q agree on the open-set π-system and on ℝ^P, hence are equal.
4. Continuity from below along V_q ↑ W gives μ₁.restrict W = μ₂.restrict W.
