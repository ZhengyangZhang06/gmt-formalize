## Proof

Source: the selected proof's notation paragraph ("on j-dimensional subspaces H^j is Lebesgue measure (isometry invariance and EuclideanSpace.euclideanHausdorffMeasure_eq_volume), so in the coordinates t^a, H^d⌞L is Lebesgue measure on ℝ^d"), and the box volumes H^d(Q_r) = (2r)^d and H^{d−1}(Q'_a) = (2r)^{d−1} of its steps 5–8.
1. (The frame isometry.) Let e : EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k) → ℝ^P, e(u) := Σ_i u_i·g_i. It is linear, and by orthonormality ‖e(u)‖² = Σ_{i,l} u_i·u_l·⟨g_i, g_l⟩ = Σ_i u_i² = ‖u‖². Hence dist(e(u), e(u')) = ‖e(u − u')‖ = ‖u − u'‖ and e is an isometry. Its range is the set of linear combinations of g_1, …, g_k, which is span(g) = G (Submodule.span_range_eq / the span of a finite family is the range of its linear-combination map).
2. (μHE on the frame space.) On EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k), μHE[k] = volume (Mathlib EuclideanSpace.euclideanHausdorffMeasure_eq_volume). By Mathlib Isometry.map_euclideanHausdorffMeasure, (μHE[k]).map e = μHE[k]⌞(range e) = H^k⌞G. Hence (volume).map e = H^k⌞G.
3. (From Fin k → ℝ.) ι = e ∘ toLp, where WithLp.toLp 2 : (Fin k → ℝ) → EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k) keeps the coordinates (so the coordinates of toLp u are u_i); toLp is measure preserving from volume to volume (Mathlib PiLp.volume_preserving_toLp). Both maps are continuous, hence measurable, so (volume).map ι = ((volume).map toLp).map e = (volume).map e = H^k⌞G (Measure.map_map). This is (i).
4. (Reduction of the boxes.) Let r > 0 and let S be one of the two boxes. S ⊆ G, and S is Borel: G is closed (a finite-dimensional subspace) and t ↦ ⟨g_i, t⟩ is continuous, so the closed box is closed and the open box is the intersection of the closed set G with an open set. Hence H^k(S) = (H^k⌞G)(S) = ((volume).map ι)(S) = volume(ι⁻¹(S)) (Measure.restrict_apply with S ∩ G = S, Measure.map_apply).
5. (Preimages.) For u : Fin k → ℝ, ι(u) ∈ G and ⟨g_i, ι(u)⟩ = Σ_l u_l·⟨g_i, g_l⟩ = u_i. Hence ι⁻¹(closed box) = {u : |u_i| ≤ r for all i}, which is the closed ball closedBall(0, r) of the sup norm of Fin k → ℝ (pi_norm_le_iff_of_nonneg), and ι⁻¹(open box) = {u : |u_i| < r for all i} = ball(0, r) (pi_norm_lt_iff, r > 0).
6. (Volumes.) By Mathlib Real.volume_pi_closedBall and Real.volume_pi_ball (r > 0), both balls have volume ENNReal.ofReal((2r)^{card (Fin k)}) = ENNReal.ofReal((2r)^k). With steps 4–5 this is (ii). ∎
(For k = 0 everything stays valid: Fin 0 → ℝ is a point, ι ≡ 0, G = {0}, both sides of (i) are the Dirac mass at 0, and both boxes are {0}, of measure 1 = (2r)^0.)

## Key steps

1. e(u) = Σ u_i g_i is a linear isometry of EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k) onto G = span g, so (μHE[k]).map e = μHE[k]⌞G, and μHE[k] = volume on EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k).
2. ι = e ∘ toLp with toLp measure preserving gives (volume).map ι = H^k⌞G.
3. The boxes lie in G and pull back under ι to sup-norm balls of radius r, of volume (2r)^k.
