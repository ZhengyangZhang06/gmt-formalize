Write η := η_{Φ(v),r}. Neither orthogonality nor a Lipschitz bound is needed.
1. (Pointwise identity.) For u ∈ ℝⁿ, Φ(u) − Φ(v) = ι(u − v) + ι'(f(u) − f(v)), so by linearity η(Φ(u)) = r⁻¹(Φ(u) − Φ(v)) = ι(r⁻¹(u − v)) + ι'(r⁻¹(f(u) − f(v))).
2. (Inclusion ⊆ in (1).) Let y = η(Φ(u)). Put w := r⁻¹(u − v); since r ≠ 0, v + rw = u, hence f_{v,r}(w) = r⁻¹(f(u) − f(v)), and Step 1 gives y = ιw + ι'(f_{v,r}(w)).
3. (Inclusion ⊇ in (1).) Given w ∈ ℝⁿ, put u := v + rw. Then r⁻¹(u − v) = w, so by Step 1 η(Φ(u)) = ιw + ι'(r⁻¹(f(v + rw) − f(v))) = ιw + ι'(f_{v,r}(w)). Steps 2–3 prove (1).
4. ((2), differentiable case.) Suppose f is differentiable at v + rw, with derivative D := Df(v + rw). The affine map w' ↦ v + rw' has derivative r·id, so by the chain rule w' ↦ f(v + rw') has derivative D∘(r·id) = rD at w; subtracting the constant f(v) does not change the derivative, and multiplying by r⁻¹ gives derivative r⁻¹·rD = D. Hence Df_{v,r}(w) = Df(v + rw).
5. ((2), non-differentiable case.) Suppose f is not differentiable at v + rw. Then f_{v,r} is not differentiable at w: for all u, f(u) = f(v) + r·f_{v,r}(r⁻¹(u − v)) (because v + r·r⁻¹(u − v) = u), and u ↦ r⁻¹(u − v) is affine and maps v + rw to w, so differentiability of f_{v,r} at w would give differentiability of f at v + rw by the chain rule. By Mathlib's convention both Fréchet derivatives are then 0 (`fderiv_zero_of_not_differentiableAt`), so again Df_{v,r}(w) = Df(v + rw). ∎
