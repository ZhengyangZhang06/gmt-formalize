## Proof

Source: parent proof Step 1 (by Lebesgue's differentiation theorem Lⁿ-a.e. v is a Lebesgue point: λ^{−n}∫_{Bⁿ_λ(v)}|· − ·(v)| → 0) and Step 2 (the substitution v' = v + λw); Simon Ch. 1, Corollary 3.17 (Lebesgue differentiation theorem, chapter-01.txt lines 929–935), here in Mathlib's form for uniformly locally doubling measures.
1. (Lebesgue points.) Lebesgue measure on ℝⁿ is an additive Haar measure on a finite-dimensional normed space, hence uniformly locally doubling and locally finite, and ℝⁿ is second countable and Borel. Since g is locally integrable, Mathlib's Lebesgue differentiation theorem (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.ae_tendsto_average_norm_sub` with constant K = 1) yields an Lⁿ-null set outside of which the following holds at v: for every filter l and centres c_j, radii δ_j with δ_j → 0⁺ along l and v ∈ closedBall(c_j, δ_j) eventually, the averages ⨍_{closedBall(c_j, δ_j)} |g(y) − g(v)| dy tend to 0 along l.
2. (Choice of balls.) Fix such v and R > 0. Apply Step 1 with l := (r → 0⁺), c_r := v and δ_r := rR: δ_r → 0⁺ because R > 0, and v ∈ closedBall(v, rR) for r > 0. Hence A(r) := ⨍_{closedBall(v, rR)} |g(y) − g(v)| dy → 0 as r ↓ 0.
3. (Change of variables.) Let r > 0 and h(y) := |g(y) − g(v)|. The dilation rule for Haar measure (Mathlib `Measure.setIntegral_comp_smul_of_pos`, applied to u ↦ h(v + u)) gives ∫_{closedBall(0,R)} h(v + rw) dw = r^{−n} ∫_{r·closedBall(0,R)} h(v + u) du, and r·closedBall(0, R) = closedBall(0, rR) since r > 0 and R ≥ 0 (`smul_closedBall`, inside `open Pointwise in`). Translation invariance of Lebesgue measure (`measurePreserving_add_left` with `MeasurePreserving.setIntegral_image_emb`, using that u ↦ v + u maps closedBall(0, rR) onto closedBall(v, rR)) gives ∫_{closedBall(0,rR)} h(v + u) du = ∫_{closedBall(v,rR)} h(y) dy.
4. (Volumes.) With c := Lⁿ(ball(0,1)) ∈ (0, ∞), Lⁿ(closedBall(v, rR)) = (rR)ⁿc and Lⁿ(closedBall(0, R)) = Rⁿc (`Measure.addHaar_closedBall`). Writing ∫_{closedBall(v,rR)} h = Lⁿ(closedBall(v, rR))·A(r) (`setAverage_eq`), Step 3 becomes ∫_{closedBall(0,R)} |g(v + rw) − g(v)| dw = r^{−n}(rR)ⁿc·A(r) = Rⁿc·A(r) = Lⁿ(closedBall(0, R))·A(r).
5. (Conclusion.) By Step 2 the right-hand side tends to Lⁿ(closedBall(0,R))·0 = 0 as r ↓ 0. Since R > 0 was arbitrary and v was any point outside the null set of Step 1, the statement holds for Lⁿ-a.e. v. ∎

## Key steps

1. Mathlib's Lebesgue differentiation theorem for the uniformly locally doubling measure Lⁿ, applied to the balls closedBall(v, rR), gives averages A(r) → 0 for a.e. v (simultaneously for all R).
2. Change variables u = rw (dilation of Haar measure) and y = v + u (translation invariance).
3. Since Lⁿ(closedBall(v, rR)) = (rR)ⁿc, the rescaled integral equals Lⁿ(closedBall(0, R))·A(r) → 0.
