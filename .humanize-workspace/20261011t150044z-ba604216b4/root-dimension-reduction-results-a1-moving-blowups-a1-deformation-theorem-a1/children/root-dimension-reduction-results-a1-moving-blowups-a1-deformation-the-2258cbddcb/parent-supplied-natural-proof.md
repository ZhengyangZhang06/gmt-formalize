A point x lies in L_j iff at least P − j of its coordinates are integers: a point of F(b, J) has the P − j integer coordinates x_i = b_i (i ∉ J); conversely if x_i ∈ ℤ for i in a set of P − j indices, let J be the complementary j indices, b_i := ⌊x_i⌋ (i ∈ J), b_i := x_i (i ∉ J). For a map f and a point x of its domain, D̄f(x) := limsup_{y→x} |f(y) − f(x)|/|y − x| (y in the domain), the pointwise upper Lipschitz constant.

A face shared by two cubes gets the same centre (a_F is defined from F alone), and translating F by z ∈ ℤ^P translates a_F by z.

## Key steps

1. Faces F(b, J), centres a_F (projection of a onto aff F, free coordinates b_i + a_i), distance > 1/4 from a_F to ∂F.
2. L*(a) ∩ F is the union of the affine planes of aff F through a_F (prescribed coordinates must be free and equal b_i + a_i), hence a cone with vertex a_F.
3. Radial retractions ψ_F map F ∖ L*(a) into ∂F ∖ L*(a), agree on common faces, and satisfy D̄ψ_F(x) ≤ (1 + 4√j)√j/|x − a_F|.
4. ψ := ψ^{(n+1)} ∘ ⋯ ∘ ψ^{(P)} maps {d > 0} into L_n, keeps each point in its closed unit cube (|ψ(x) − x| ≤ √P) and is ℤ^P-periodic.
5. Induction on ℓ = P − n: D̄ψ ≤ c₁/d, via the (P − 1)-face induction hypothesis, the split L = L₀ ∪ L₁ (translated planes at distance ≥ 1/4), homogeneity of K₀ = p_F^{-1}(L₀) ⊆ L*(a) about a, and the corrected bound |ψ_C(x) − a| > 1/4 (Simon prints 3/4).
6. Segment argument for |x − y| < r/2 and the bound 2√P + |x − y| otherwise give the Lipschitz constant (2c₁ + 8√P + 1)/r on {d ≥ r}.
