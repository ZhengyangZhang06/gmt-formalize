## Proof

Source: parent proof, Parts 1.1, 1.3 and 2.1 (Simon Ch. 3, Lemma 1.2 and Remark 1.3, chapter-03.txt lines 25-73, and the procedure of the area formula, lines 376-381, invoked for the co-area formula at lines 426-428 and for the slices at lines 440-442; C¹ Approximation Theorem 1.5 and Rademacher's Theorem 1.4 of Ch. 2). Departures from the parent's 1.1/1.3, stated explicitly: ψ_α is only required to be Lipschitz (not bi-Lipschitz) on D_α, which is all the later steps use; the agreement of derivatives is recorded as the Fréchet derivative of f∘ψ_α at u (the parent's Dh_α(u) = DG(u)); and E_{α,i} does not remove the exceptional set Z_α of the chart lemma, which is instead handled where it is used (siblings chart_piece_coarea_formula and coarea_tangent_existence). No measurability of M is needed here.
1. (Submanifold cover.) By the forward direction of SubmanifoldCoverCharacterization (for n, ℓ, M), M ⊆ N₀ ∪ ⋃_{i∈ℕ} N_i with Hⁿ(N₀) = 0 and each N_i satisfying IsC1Submanifold n: every y ∈ N_i has V ⊆ ℝⁿ, W ∋ y open and ψ : ℝⁿ → ℝ^{n+ℓ} with ψ C¹ and injective on V, ψ(V) = W ∩ N_i and Dψ(x) injective for x ∈ V.
2. (Countably many submanifold charts.) Fix i. The open sets W (one chosen for each y ∈ N_i) cover N_i; ℝ^{n+ℓ} is second countable, so countably many of them, with charts (V_{i,r}, ψ_{i,r}), r ∈ ℕ, cover N_i (Lindelöf).
3. (Good balls inside one chart.) Fix a chart (V, ψ) from step 2 and put V' := V ∩ ψ⁻¹(U), open since ψ is continuous on the open V and U is open. For u ∈ V', x := ψ(u) ∈ U; local Lipschitz continuity of f on the open U gives s > 0 with B_s(x) ⊆ U and f Lipschitz on B_s(x). By continuity of ψ at u choose r_u > 0 with closedBall(u, r_u) ⊆ V' and ψ(closedBall(u, r_u)) ⊆ B_s(x). Dψ is continuous on V (ψ is C¹ on the open V), hence bounded on the compact convex closedBall(u, r_u) ⊆ V, so ψ is Lipschitz on it by the mean value inequality (Mathlib ContDiffOn.exists_lipschitzOnWith / Convex.lipschitzOnWith_of_nnnorm_fderivWithin_le); put D := B_{r_u}(u). Then ψ is C¹, injective and Lipschitz on D with injective derivative, and h := f ∘ ψ is Lipschitz on D (composition, ψ(D) ⊆ B_s(x)). By Lindelöf, countably many of the balls B_{r_u}(u), u ∈ V', cover V'.
4. (Enumeration and coverage.) Collecting the balls of step 3 over all charts of step 2 and all i (and padding with empty charts D = ∅, ψ = 0 if necessary) gives (D_α, ψ_α), α ∈ ℕ, with the chart properties of the statement and h_α := f ∘ ψ_α Lipschitz on D_α. If x ∈ M \ N₀, then x ∈ N_i for some i, x ∈ W_{i,r} ∩ N_i = ψ_{i,r}(V_{i,r}) for some r, so x = ψ_{i,r}(u) with u ∈ V_{i,r}, and u ∈ V'_{i,r} because x ∈ M ⊆ U; hence u lies in one of the chosen balls. Thus M \ N₀ ⊆ ⋃_α ψ_α(D_α).
5. (C¹ approximation; parent 1.3.) Fix α. Extend h_α|_{D_α} to a Lipschitz map h̃_α : ℝⁿ → ℝᵐ (coordinatewise McShane extension, Simon Ch. 2, 1.2; SimonGMT exists_lipschitz_extension_finiteDimensional). For i ≥ 1, (C1) with ε = 1/i gives G_{α,i} of class C¹ with Lⁿ(Bad_{α,i}) < 1/i, Bad_{α,i} := {h̃_α ≠ G_{α,i}} ∪ {Dh̃_α ≠ DG_{α,i}}. Put E_{α,i} := D_α ∩ Diff(h̃_α) ∩ {h̃_α = G_{α,i}} ∩ {Dh̃_α = DG_{α,i}}, Diff(h̃_α) the set of differentiability points of h̃_α. E_{α,i} is Borel: Diff(h̃_α) is Borel (measurableSet_of_differentiableAt), {h̃_α = G_{α,i}} is closed, and {Dh̃_α = DG_{α,i}} is Borel because u ↦ Dh̃_α(u) and u ↦ DG_{α,i}(u) are Borel maps (measurable_fderiv) into the finite-dimensional, hence second-countable, space of continuous linear maps, whose diagonal is measurable (measurableSet_eq_fun).
6. (Agreement to first order.) Let u ∈ E_{α,i}. Then f(ψ_α(u)) = h_α(u) = h̃_α(u) = G_{α,i}(u). Since D_α is open and h̃_α = f ∘ ψ_α on D_α, the two maps coincide on a neighbourhood of u; h̃_α is differentiable at u with Dh̃_α(u) = DG_{α,i}(u); hence f ∘ ψ_α has Fréchet derivative DG_{α,i}(u) at u.
7. (Full measure in each chart.) D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i} ⊆ (ℝⁿ \ Diff(h̃_α)) ∪ ⋂_i Bad_{α,i}. The first set is Lⁿ-null by Rademacher's theorem (h̃_α is Lipschitz; Mathlib LipschitzWith.ae_differentiableAt), and Lⁿ(⋂_i Bad_{α,i}) ≤ inf_i 1/i = 0. So Lⁿ(D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i}) = 0.
8. (Null images.) ψ_α is Lipschitz on D_α, so Hⁿ(ψ_α(D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i})) ≤ Lip(ψ_α)ⁿ·Hⁿ(D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i}) = Lip(ψ_α)ⁿ·Lⁿ(D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i}) = 0 (SimonGMT euclideanHausdorffMeasure_image_le_of_lipschitzOn; Hⁿ = Lⁿ on ℝⁿ by EuclideanSpace.euclideanHausdorffMeasure_eq_volume).
9. (Conclusion.) Re-index the pairs (α, i) by j ∈ ℕ through a bijection ℕ × ℕ ≅ ℕ and set D_j := D_α, ψ_j := ψ_α, G_j := G_{α,i}, E_j := E_{α,i}; steps 3-6 give all the listed properties. If x ∈ M \ N₀ then x = ψ_α(u) with u ∈ D_α (step 4); either u ∈ E_{α,i} for some i, so x ∈ ⋃_j ψ_j(E_j), or u ∈ D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i}. Hence M \ ⋃_j ψ_j(E_j) ⊆ N₀ ∪ ⋃_α ψ_α(D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i}), a countable union of Hⁿ-null sets (steps 1, 8). ∎

## Key steps

1. Lemma 1.2 gives M ⊆ N₀ ∪ ⋃ N_i with C¹ submanifolds N_i; Lindelöf gives countably many submanifold charts.
2. Inside each chart choose countably many small balls D_α (closure in the chart, image inside U where f is Lipschitz): ψ_α is C¹, injective, Lipschitz with injective derivative and f∘ψ_α is Lipschitz; M \ N₀ ⊆ ⋃ ψ_α(D_α).
3. Lipschitz extension of f∘ψ_α and C¹ approximation (C1) with ε = 1/i; Borel agreement sets E_{α,i} where values and derivatives agree.
4. Rademacher: D_α \ ⋃_i E_{α,i} is Lⁿ-null, so its ψ_α-image is Hⁿ-null.
5. Re-index (α, i) by ℕ.
