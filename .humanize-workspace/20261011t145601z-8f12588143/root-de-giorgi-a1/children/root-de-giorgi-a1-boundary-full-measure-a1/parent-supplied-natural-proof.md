## Proof

Source. Parent proof Part 5a; Simon, Ch. 3, chapter-03.txt line 584 ("Since |ν| = 1 μ_E-a.e. in U, by virtue of Theorem 3.23 of Ch. 1 we have μ_E(U ∖ ∂*E) = 0"), i.e. the Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentiation theorem for Radon measures on ℝ^N (Simon Ch. 1, Corollary 3.17 / Theorem 3.23, via the Besicovitch covering lemma; Mathlib Besicovitch.vitaliFamily, Besicovitch.tendsto_filterAt, VitaliFamily.ae_tendsto_average).

Notation. B_ρ(y) open ball, B̄_r(y) closed ball; ⨍_A f dλ := λ(A).toReal⁻¹ • ∫_A f dλ; then normalAverage μ ν y ρ = ⨍_{B_ρ(y)} ν dμ. The argument is uniform in N (for N = 0 it also applies verbatim).

Step 1 (localization). For integers i ≥ 1 let W_i := {y : |y| < i, infEdist(y, Uᶜ) > 1/i} (with infEdist(y, ∅) = ∞). W_i is open; its closure lies in the compact set {|y| ≤ i, infEdist(y, Uᶜ) ≥ 1/i}, which is contained in U because Uᶜ is closed; and ⋃_i W_i = U. Let μ_i := μ.restrict W_i: a finite Borel measure on ℝ^N (μ(W_i) ≤ μ(closure W_i) < ∞), hence locally finite.

Step 2 (Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentiation). ν is μ_i-a.e. strongly measurable and |ν| ≤ 1 μ_i-a.e., so ν ∈ L¹(μ_i). By the Besicovitch differentiation theorem on ℝ^N, applied to the Vitali family of closed balls of μ_i (VitaliFamily.ae_tendsto_average for Besicovitch.vitaliFamily μ_i, together with Besicovitch.tendsto_filterAt), for μ_i-a.e. y: ⨍_{B̄_r(y)} ν dμ_i → ν(y) as r ↓ 0. Choose a measurable μ_i-null set containing the exceptional points; its intersection with W_i, together with the μ-null set {y : |ν(y)| ≠ 1}, is a μ-null set Z_i such that every y ∈ W_i ∖ Z_i satisfies this convergence and |ν(y)| = 1.

Step 3 (from closed to open balls). Fix y ∈ W_i ∖ Z_i and δ₀ > 0 with B̄_{δ₀}(y) ⊆ W_i. For r ≤ δ₀, μ_i and μ agree on subsets of B̄_r(y), so ⨍_{B̄_r(y)} ν dμ → ν(y) as r ↓ 0. Given ε ∈ (0, 1), choose δ ∈ (0, δ₀] with |⨍_{B̄_r(y)} ν dμ − ν(y)| ≤ ε for r ∈ (0, δ). Since |ν(y)| = 1 > ε, these averages are nonzero, hence μ(B̄_r(y)) > 0 for r ∈ (0, δ) (if μ(B̄_r(y)) = 0 the average is 0). Let ρ ∈ (0, δ). As r ↑ ρ, B̄_r(y) ↑ B_ρ(y) (the open ball is the increasing union of the smaller closed balls), so μ(B̄_r(y)) → μ(B_ρ(y)) ∈ (0, ∞) and ∫_{B̄_r(y)} ν dμ → ∫_{B_ρ(y)} ν dμ (dominated convergence; finite measure). Hence normalAverage μ ν y ρ = ⨍_{B_ρ(y)} ν dμ = lim_{r↑ρ} ⨍_{B̄_r(y)} ν dμ, and |normalAverage μ ν y ρ − ν(y)| ≤ ε. Therefore normalAverage μ ν y ρ → ν(y) as ρ ↓ 0, with |ν(y)| = 1 and y ∈ W_i ⊆ U; that is, y ∈ reducedBoundary U μ ν.

Step 4 (conclusion). By Step 3, ℝ^N ∖ reducedBoundary U μ ν ⊆ Uᶜ ∪ ⋃_i Z_i. Since μ(Uᶜ) = 0 and μ(Z_i) = 0, countable subadditivity (of the outer measure; no measurability of the reduced boundary is needed) gives μ(ℝ^N ∖ reducedBoundary U μ ν) = 0. ∎

## Key steps

1. Exhaust U by open sets W_i with compact closure in U; μ⌞W_i is a finite measure and ν ∈ L¹(μ⌞W_i).
2. Besicovitch-Lebesgue differentiation: for μ-a.e. y ∈ W_i, closed-ball averages of ν converge to ν(y), and |ν(y)| = 1.
3. Open-ball averages are limits of closed-ball averages of smaller radii (with μ(B̄_r(y)) > 0), so normalAverage(y, ρ) → ν(y), a unit vector: y ∈ ∂*E.
4. Countable union of the μ-null exceptional sets together with μ(Uᶜ) = 0.
